Nam Sot Pad

I now work almost exclusively from home, which also means that I can cook something delicious during my lunch break almost every day. Of course, it needs to be quick, so Thai stir-fries appear on the table very often. At some point I noticed that they almost always follow a similar pattern and are usually seasoned with a mixture of oyster sauce, soy sauce, fish sauce, and Golden Mountain Sauce.

In Pailin Chongchitnant’s book “Sabai”, I eventually came across the “Universal Stir-Fry Sauce”, which consists of exactly this mixture of sauces. According to Pailin, this mixture, called “Sot Pad” ( ) or “Nam Sot Pad” ( ), is prepared in advance in many Thai restaurants to save time when cooking stir-fries.

This sauce serves as a base that can be used as it is or, depending on the dish, supplemented with other ingredients such as white pepper or yellow bean paste. There is actually almost always a pinch of sugar involved too, and I wondered why Pailin did not add it directly. Her answer is as simple as it is convincing: sugar only dissolves well when heated, and the “Nam Sot Pad” is mixed cold.

I have nothing against saving even more time while cooking during my lunch break, so I have now mixed my own “Nam Sot Pad”, filled it into a practical squeeze bottle with a lid, and had Anne design a label for it. I have written down the “recipe” for the sauce, along with ten quick stir-fries I have tested myself.

Ingredients for 240ml (8.1 fl oz) sauce

  • 120ml (4.1 fl oz) oyster sauce
  • 60ml (2 fl oz) light soy sauce
  • 30ml (1 fl oz) fish sauce
  • 30ml (1 fl oz) Golden Mountain Sauce

The following recipes are only meant as a starting point for your own journey of discovery into the aromatic world of stir-fries. In theory, you can use the sauce to season all kinds of ingredients and create your own dishes with meat, seafood, mushrooms, or simply your favorite vegetables. And if you want to make it fully vegetarian, you can replace the fish sauce in the “Nam Sot Pad” with a vegan “no-fish sauce”.

For all recipes, I marinated the already sliced meat in the morning before cooking, using a mixture of soy sauce, cornstarch, and sesame oil. This velveting makes the meat more tender and, at least in my opinion, more aromatic.

Gai Pad Hed

We start with “Gai Pad Hed” ( ), a dish with chicken and various mushrooms, such as king oyster mushrooms, shiitake, and straw mushrooms.

Ingredients for 2 servings

  • 200g (7.1 oz) chicken breast fillet
  • 200g (7.1 oz) mixed mushrooms
  • one clove of garlic
  • one spring onion
  • 2-3 tbsp Nam Sot Pad
  • 1/4 tsp palm sugar
  • 1-2 tbsp vegetable oil

Preparation

  1. Wash the meat, pat it dry, cut it into bite-sized pieces, and velvet it
  2. Peel and finely chop the garlic
  3. Clean all mushrooms and cut them into bite-sized pieces
  4. Cut the spring onion into rings, separating the white and green parts
  5. Heat the oil in a wok
  6. Fry the garlic until fragrant
  7. Fry the meat until it has changed color
  8. Add the mushrooms and the white part of the spring onion
  9. Deglaze with Nam Sot Pad and season with palm sugar
  10. Simmer until the mushrooms are cooked
  11. Add the green parts of the spring onion
  12. Serve with jasmine rice

Gai Pad Normai Farang

For “Gai Pad Normai Farang” ( ), marinated chicken is stir-fried with green asparagus. In Thai, asparagus is called “Normai Farang”, which means something like “foreign bamboo”.

Ingredients for 2 servings

  • 200g (7.1 oz) chicken breast fillet
  • 200g (7.1 oz) green asparagus
  • one clove of garlic
  • 2-3 tbsp Nam Sot Pad
  • 1/4 tsp palm sugar
  • 1-2 tbsp vegetable oil

Preparation

  1. Wash the meat, pat it dry, cut it into bite-sized pieces, and velvet it
  2. Cut the asparagus into bite-sized pieces and blanch it
  3. Peel and finely chop the garlic
  4. Heat the oil in a wok
  5. Fry the garlic until fragrant
  6. Fry the meat until it has changed color
  7. Add the asparagus pieces
  8. Deglaze everything with Nam Sot Pad and season with palm sugar
  9. Simmer until the asparagus is cooked
  10. Serve with jasmine rice

Gai Pad Normai

“Gai Pad Normai” ( ) sounds very similar to the previous dish. That is because this one uses “real” bamboo in the form of bamboo shoots. If you use raw bamboo shoots for any reason, be sure to boil them first so that the toxic hydrocyanic acid is broken down. Usually, though, the only bamboo shoots available here are already boiled strips from a tin. That is what I used here.

Ingredients for 2 servings

  • 250g (8.8 oz) chicken breast fillet
  • 150g (5.3 oz) bamboo shoots
  • one clove of garlic
  • 2 Thai chillies
  • 2-3 tbsp Nam Sot Pad
  • 1/4 tsp palm sugar
  • 1-2 tbsp vegetable oil

Preparation

  1. Wash the meat, pat it dry, cut it into bite-sized pieces, and velvet it
  2. Peel and finely chop the garlic
  3. Cut the Thai chillies into rings
  4. Heat the oil in a wok
  5. Fry the garlic until fragrant
  6. Fry the meat until it has completely changed color
  7. Add the bamboo shoots and chilli rings
  8. Deglaze everything with Nam Sot Pad and season with palm sugar
  9. Simmer until the bamboo shoots are fully heated through
  10. Serve with jasmine rice

Gai Pad Pak Choi

For “Gai Pad Pak Choi” ( ), the meat is stir-fried with pak choi this time. Instead of pak choi, you can of course use another type of mustard cabbage. In any case, it is important that the vegetables still retain a little bite.

Ingredients for 2 servings

  • 250g (8.8 oz) chicken breast fillet
  • 150g (5.3 oz) baby pak choi
  • one clove of garlic
  • two Thai chillies
  • 2-3 tbsp Nam Sot Pad
  • 1/4 tsp palm sugar
  • 1-2 tbsp vegetable oil

Preparation

  1. Wash the meat, pat it dry, cut it into bite-sized pieces, and velvet it
  2. Wash the pak choi, separate the stems from the leaves, and cut the leaves into more bite-sized pieces if needed
  3. Peel and finely chop the garlic
  4. Cut the Thai chillies into rings
  5. Heat the oil in a wok
  6. Fry the garlic until fragrant
  7. Fry the meat until it has completely changed color
  8. Add the pak choi stems and chilli rings
  9. Deglaze with Nam Sot Pad and season with palm sugar
  10. Simmer briefly, then fold in the leaves
  11. Serve with jasmine rice

Moo Pad Saam Sahai

“Moo Pad Saam Sahai” ( ) is the first dish in this series prepared with a simple paste. It consists of garlic, coriander roots, and white pepper and is also called “Saam Sahai” or “Saam Gloe”, both of which mean “Three Friends”. The name probably comes from the fact that these three ingredients harmonize so well.

Ingredients for 2 servings

  • 200g (7.1 oz) pork neck
  • 100g (3.5 oz) baby corn
  • 100g (3.5 oz) broccoli
  • one spring onion
  • one clove of garlic
  • two coriander roots
  • 8 white peppercorns
  • 2-3 tbsp Nam Sot Pad
  • 1/4 tsp palm sugar
  • 1-2 tbsp vegetable oil

Preparation

  1. Wash the meat, pat it dry, cut it into bite-sized pieces, and velvet it
  2. Cut the baby corn cobs into diagonal pieces
  3. Cut off small broccoli florets and blanch them briefly
  4. Cut the spring onion into rings
  5. Peel the garlic and cut it into pieces, then cut the coriander roots into pieces
  6. Pound the garlic, coriander roots, and pepper in a mortar into a coarse paste
  7. Heat the oil in a wok
  8. Fry the paste until fragrant
  9. Fry the meat until it has completely changed color
  10. Add the baby corn and broccoli
  11. Deglaze with Nam Sot Pad and season with palm sugar
  12. Add the spring onion
  13. Simmer briefly, but the vegetables should still stay crisp-tender
  14. Serve with jasmine rice

Nuea Pad Pak Choi

For the next dish, we change the type of meat again and stir-fry beef with pak choi. For “Nuea Pad Pak Choi” ( ), however, we only sear the meat briefly over high heat. The baby pak choi I used here had just started to flower. You can eat the flowers without any concern.

Ingredients for 2 servings

  • 250g (8.8 oz) beef steak
  • 150g (5.3 oz) baby pak choi
  • one clove of garlic
  • two Thai chillies
  • 2-3 tbsp Nam Sot Pad
  • 1/4 tsp palm sugar
  • 1-2 tbsp vegetable oil

Preparation

  1. Cut the meat into bite-sized pieces and velvet it
  2. Wash the pak choi, then separate the stems from the leaves and cut them into more bite-sized pieces if needed
  3. Peel and finely chop the garlic
  4. Cut the Thai chillies into rings
  5. Heat the oil in a wok
  6. Fry the garlic until fragrant
  7. Sear the meat over high heat
  8. Add the pak choi stems and chilli rings
  9. Deglaze with Nam Sot Pad and season with palm sugar
  10. Simmer briefly, then fold in the leaves
  11. Serve with jasmine rice

Nuea Pad Thakrai

For “Nuea Pad Thakrai” ( ), another simple paste is used in addition to the “Nam Sot Pad”. This time it is made from garlic, chillies, and lemongrass. We stay with beef, and this time there are no vegetables.

Ingredients for 2 servings

  • 350g (12.3 oz) beef steak
  • two cloves of garlic
  • 2-3 Thai chillies
  • one stalk of lemongrass
  • 2 tbsp Nam Sot Pad
  • 1-2 tbsp vegetable oil

Preparation

  1. Cut the meat into bite-sized pieces and velvet it
  2. Peel the garlic and cut it into pieces
  3. Cut the Thai chillies into pieces
  4. Remove the dry ends and outer layer from the lemongrass, then cut the rest into rings
  5. Pound the garlic, Thai chillies, and lemongrass in a mortar into a coarse paste
  6. Heat the oil in a wok
  7. Fry the paste until fragrant
  8. Add the meat and sear it in the paste over high heat
  9. Deglaze with Nam Sot Pad
  10. Simmer briefly until all the aromas have combined
  11. Serve with jasmine rice

Pad Woon Sen Normai Farang

For the next dish, we skip the meat and use glass noodles and eggs instead. As the vegetable, “Pad Woon Sen Normai Farang” ( ) once again uses green asparagus. What may seem unusual to us is that this dish is also served with rice.

Ingredients for 2 servings

  • 200g (7.1 oz) glass noodles
  • 4-5 green asparagus spears
  • 3 eggs
  • one clove of garlic
  • 2-3 tbsp Nam Sot Pad
  • 2 tbsp vegetable oil

Preparation

  1. Soak the noodles in hot water according to the packet instructions, then drain them thoroughly
  2. Cut the asparagus into bite-sized pieces and blanch them
  3. Peel and finely chop the garlic
  4. Crack the eggs and whisk them
  5. Heat two thirds of the oil in a wok
  6. Fry the garlic until fragrant
  7. Add the asparagus and briefly stir-fry it
  8. Add the glass noodles and mix them with the asparagus
  9. Push everything to one side of the wok, add a little oil to the free area, and fry the eggs
  10. As soon as they begin to set, break them up and mix them with the remaining ingredients
  11. Deglaze with Nam Sot Pad and mix everything well
  12. Serve with jasmine rice

Pad Galam Plee Moo Sap

For “Pad Galam Plee Moo Sap” ( ), we use minced meat and choi sam. This leafy vegetable is another type of mustard cabbage, but you can also replace it with pak choi or even broccoli.

Ingredients for 2 servings

  • 200g (7.1 oz) minced pork
  • 200g (7.1 oz) choi sam
  • one clove of garlic
  • 4-5 Thai chillies
  • 2-3 tbsp Nam Sot Pad
  • 1/4 tsp palm sugar
  • 1-2 tbsp vegetable oil

Preparation

  1. Wash the choi sam, separate the stems from the leaves, and cut the leaves and stems into more bite-sized pieces
  2. Peel and finely chop the garlic
  3. Cut the Thai chillies into rings
  4. Heat the oil in a wok
  5. Fry the garlic until fragrant
  6. Fry the minced meat until it has completely changed color
  7. Add the choi sam stems and chilli rings
  8. Deglaze with Nam Sot Pad and season with palm sugar
  9. Simmer briefly, then fold in the leaves
  10. Cook the leaves very briefly
  11. Serve with jasmine rice

Pad Moo Sap Makuea Prau

For the last dish in this series, we stir-fry minced meat once more. For “Pad Moo Sap Makuea Prau” ( ), it is stir-fried with Thai eggplants and mild chillies.

Ingredients for 2 servings

  • 200g (7.1 oz) minced pork
  • 5-6 small Thai eggplants
  • one clove of garlic
  • 2 large, mild Thai chillies
  • 3-4 tbsp Nam Sot Pad
  • 1/4 tsp palm sugar
  • 2 kaffir lime leaves
  • 1-2 tbsp vegetable oil

Preparation

  1. Quarter the Thai eggplants, blanch them briefly, and place them in cold water so they do not turn brown
  2. Peel and finely chop the garlic
  3. Cut the Thai chillies into diagonal rings
  4. Tear the kaffir lime leaves into pieces
  5. Heat the oil in a wok
  6. Fry the garlic until fragrant
  7. Fry the minced meat until it has completely changed color
  8. Add the Thai eggplants and kaffir lime leaves
  9. Deglaze with Nam Sot Pad and season with palm sugar
  10. Simmer briefly until the eggplants are no longer hard
  11. Fold in the Thai chillies and cook them very briefly
  12. Serve with jasmine rice
